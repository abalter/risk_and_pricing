The separability lemma

Notation

  • \(V\) — the rating variables; \(t \subseteq V\) is a set of variables
  • \(M \subseteq \mathcal{P}(V)\) — the sets that carry a term in the model
  • \(g_t(x_t)\) — the term on \(t\); \(\beta_t = \ln g_t\) is its coefficient table
  • \(T \in M\) — the term whose table we want; \(\bar{T} = V \setminus T\)
  • \(x\) — a row; \(z_T\) — chosen values for the variables in \(T\); \(z^0\) — base values
  • \((z_T;\, x_{\bar{T}})\) — the row with \(T\) fixed at \(z\) and everything else left as it is
  • \(E_P[\,\cdot\,]\) — exposure-weighted mean over the rows of a data set \(P\)

Three relations to \(T\)

Every set of variables \(t \subseteq V\) stands in exactly one relation to \(T\):

\[ \begin{aligned} t \subseteq T &&&\text{inside } T\\ t \cap T = \emptyset,\quad t \neq \emptyset &&&\text{disjoint from } T\\ t \cap T \neq \emptyset,\quad t \not\subseteq T &&&\text{overlapping } T \text{ partway} \end{aligned} \]

This partitions \(\mathcal{P}(V)\). The model’s terms inherit it:

\[ Q = M \cap \mathcal{P}(T), \qquad Q' = \{\,t \in M : t \cap T = \emptyset,\ t \neq \emptyset\,\}, \qquad Q'' = M \setminus (Q \cup Q') \]

Example. Terms age, sex, breed, weight, deductible, age×weight, breed×weight; \(T = \{\text{age}\}\). \(Q = \{\emptyset, \text{age}\}\), \(\;Q' = \{\text{sex}, \text{breed}, \text{weight}, \text{deductible}, \text{breed×weight}\}\), \(\;Q'' = \{\text{age×weight}\}\).

The model, term by term

\[ \ln \hat{\mu}(x) = \underbrace{\beta_0}_{\text{intercept}} + \underbrace{\sum_{|t| = 1} \beta_t(x_t)}_{\text{main effects}} + \underbrace{\sum_{|t| \geq 2} \beta_t(x_t)}_{\text{interactions}} \]

As a product:

\[ \hat{\mu}(x) = \prod_{t \in M} g_t(x_t), \qquad g_t = e^{\beta_t}, \qquad g_\emptyset = e^{\beta_0} \]

Each factor sees only its own variables.

Fix \(T\), sort the terms by overlap

Evaluate at \((z_T;\, x_{\bar{T}})\). A term in \(Q\) sees only fixed values; a term in \(Q'\) sees only the row’s values; a term in \(Q''\) sees both:

\[ \hat{\mu}(z_T;\, x_{\bar{T}}) = \underbrace{\prod_{t \in Q} g_t(z_t)}_{G_Q(z)} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{\prod_{t \in Q'} g_t(x_t)}_{G_{Q'}(x)} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{\prod_{t \in Q''} g_t(z_{t \cap T};\, x_{t \setminus T})}_{G_{Q''}(z,\, x)} \]

Only \(G_{Q''}\) mixes \(z\) and \(x\).

If \(Q'' = \emptyset\): average over any \(P\), take the ratio to base, and \(G_{Q'}\) cancels — \(P\) with it.

What \(Q''\) depends on, and how to pin it

\[ C = \bigcup_{t \in Q''} (t \setminus T) \qquad \text{the variables } Q'' \text{ reaches outside } T \]

Hold them at base, \(x_C = z^0_C\):

\[ \hat{\mu}(z_T;\, z^0_C;\, x_{\overline{T \cup C}}) = \underbrace{\prod_{t \in Q} g_t(z_t) \cdot \prod_{t \in Q''} g_t(z_{t \cap T};\, z^0_{t \setminus T})}_{z \text{ only}} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{\prod_{t \in Q'} g_t(z^0_{t \cap C};\, x_{t \setminus C})}_{x \text{ only}} \]

  • Every \(Q''\) term is now a function of \(z\) alone; under treatment contrasts each equals 1
  • \(Q'\) terms were never functions of \(z\), and still aren’t
  • Average over any \(P\), take the ratio to base: the \(x\)-only product cancels, and \(P\) is gone

Example: \(A\), \(B\), \(C\), \(AB\), \(BC\)

\[ \ln \hat{\mu}(a, b, c) = \beta_0 + \beta_A(a) + \beta_B(b) + \beta_C(c) + \beta_{AB}(a, b) + \beta_{BC}(b, c) \]

\[ \hat{\mu}(a, b, c) = g_\emptyset\, g_A(a)\, g_B(b)\, g_C(c)\, g_{AB}(a, b)\, g_{BC}(b, c) \]

Fix \(T\); the variables not in \(T\) keep their row values \(x_A, x_B, x_C\):

\[ \begin{aligned} T = \{A\}:\quad \hat{\mu}(a, x_B, x_C) &= \underbrace{g_\emptyset\, g_A(a)}_{Q} \cdot \underbrace{g_B(x_B)\, g_C(x_C)\, g_{BC}(x_B, x_C)}_{Q'} \cdot \underbrace{g_{AB}(a, x_B)}_{Q''}\\[4pt] T = \{B\}:\quad \hat{\mu}(x_A, b, x_C) &= \underbrace{g_\emptyset\, g_B(b)}_{Q} \cdot \underbrace{g_A(x_A)\, g_C(x_C)}_{Q'} \cdot \underbrace{g_{AB}(x_A, b)\, g_{BC}(b, x_C)}_{Q''}\\[4pt] T = \{C\}:\quad \hat{\mu}(x_A, x_B, c) &= \underbrace{g_\emptyset\, g_C(c)}_{Q} \cdot \underbrace{g_A(x_A)\, g_B(x_B)\, g_{AB}(x_A, x_B)}_{Q'} \cdot \underbrace{g_{BC}(x_B, c)}_{Q''}\\[4pt] T = \{A, B\}:\quad \hat{\mu}(a, b, x_C) &= \underbrace{g_\emptyset\, g_A(a)\, g_B(b)\, g_{AB}(a, b)}_{Q} \cdot \underbrace{g_C(x_C)}_{Q'} \cdot \underbrace{g_{BC}(b, x_C)}_{Q''}\\[4pt] T = \{B, C\}:\quad \hat{\mu}(x_A, b, c) &= \underbrace{g_\emptyset\, g_B(b)\, g_C(c)\, g_{BC}(b, c)}_{Q} \cdot \underbrace{g_A(x_A)}_{Q'} \cdot \underbrace{g_{AB}(x_A, b)}_{Q''} \end{aligned} \]

Recovering the coefficients

Pin the variables that \(Q''\) reaches outside \(T\) at base, average over the rest, take the ratio to base. Treatment contrasts throughout.

Main effects:

\[ \begin{aligned} e^{\beta_A(a)} &= \frac{E_P\big[\hat{\mu}(a, b_0, x_C)\big]}{E_P\big[\hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, x_C)\big]} &&\text{pin } b\\[6pt] e^{\beta_B(b)} &= \frac{\hat{\mu}(a_0, b, c_0)}{\hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, c_0)} &&\text{pin } a \text{ and } c \text{ — nothing left to average}\\[6pt] e^{\beta_C(c)} &= \frac{E_P\big[\hat{\mu}(x_A, b_0, c)\big]}{E_P\big[\hat{\mu}(x_A, b_0, c_0)\big]} &&\text{pin } b \end{aligned} \]

Interactions: the pinned joint table, then the alternating product over its corners:

\[ \begin{aligned} R_{AB}(a, b) &= \frac{\hat{\mu}(a, b, c_0)}{\hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, c_0)}, & e^{\beta_{AB}(a, b)} &= \frac{R_{AB}(a, b)\; R_{AB}(a_0, b_0)}{R_{AB}(a, b_0)\; R_{AB}(a_0, b)} &&\text{pin } c\\[6pt] R_{BC}(b, c) &= \frac{\hat{\mu}(a_0, b, c)}{\hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, c_0)}, & e^{\beta_{BC}(b, c)} &= \frac{R_{BC}(b, c)\; R_{BC}(b_0, c_0)}{R_{BC}(b, c_0)\; R_{BC}(b_0, c)} &&\text{pin } a \end{aligned} \]

The same, on the log scale

Main effects: one plus, one minus.

\[ \begin{aligned} \beta_A(a) &= \ln E_P\big[\hat{\mu}(a, b_0, x_C)\big] - \ln E_P\big[\hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, x_C)\big]\\[4pt] \beta_B(b) &= \ln \hat{\mu}(a_0, b, c_0) - \ln \hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, c_0)\\[4pt] \beta_C(c) &= \ln E_P\big[\hat{\mu}(x_A, b_0, c)\big] - \ln E_P\big[\hat{\mu}(x_A, b_0, c_0)\big] \end{aligned} \]

Interactions: sign \((-1)^{\text{number of coordinates at base}}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \beta_{AB}(a, b) &= \ln R_{AB}(a, b) - \ln R_{AB}(a, b_0) - \ln R_{AB}(a_0, b) + \ln R_{AB}(a_0, b_0)\\ &= \ln \hat{\mu}(a, b, c_0) - \ln \hat{\mu}(a, b_0, c_0) - \ln \hat{\mu}(a_0, b, c_0) + \ln \hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, c_0)\\[8pt] \beta_{BC}(b, c) &= \ln R_{BC}(b, c) - \ln R_{BC}(b, c_0) - \ln R_{BC}(b_0, c) + \ln R_{BC}(b_0, c_0)\\ &= \ln \hat{\mu}(a_0, b, c) - \ln \hat{\mu}(a_0, b, c_0) - \ln \hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, c) + \ln \hat{\mu}(a_0, b_0, c_0) \end{aligned} \]

From a GLM with treatment contrasts

The fit has done all of it.

Treatment coding makes \(g_t = 1\) whenever any coordinate of \(t\) is at base. So at the base cell every \(Q''\) term is already 1, for every \(T\), and

\[ \text{table}_t(z_t) = e^{\beta_t(z_t)} \qquad \text{for every } t \in M \]

Read the tables off the coefficients. No data, no averaging, no choice of \(P\).

What the lemma adds:

  • The same tables come back from the recipe on the next slide applied to predict(). The coefficients are that recipe, done in advance by the parameterization.
  • A partial-dependence table for a variable agrees with \(e^{\beta}\) only when the variable sits in no interaction (\(Q'' = \emptyset\)). Otherwise the two differ, on the training data too, and the coefficient is the rating table.
  • Other contrasts (sum-to-zero, Helmert) give the same \(\hat{\mu}\) in other coordinates. Refit with treatment contrasts, or use the recipe.

From a GAM, a penalized fit, random effects

Same term list, but the fit chose the gauge.

s(age) + s(weight) + ti(age, weight) + s(breed, bs = "re") has a term list \(M\). But the smooths are centered on the training data, ti() is centered on the training data, random effects are centered at zero. Nothing is 1 at a base level, and predict(type = "terms") returns these centered pieces: for a \(T\) with \(Q'' \neq \emptyset\) (age, because of ti(age, weight)) the centered main effect carries a training-book average of the interaction.

Recipe — only predict(), never the coefficients:

  1. Choose a base cell \(z^0\): a well-populated level of each variable. Random effects have no reference level, so this choice is yours.
  2. For each \(t \in M\), hold everything outside \(t\) at base, vary \(t\)’s variables, and take the alternating product over the corners of \(t\):

\[ \text{table}_t(z_t) = \prod_{u \subseteq t} \hat{\mu}\big(z^{(u)}\big)^{(-1)^{|t \setminus u|}}, \qquad z^{(u)} = \text{the base cell with the variables in } u \text{ moved to } z \]

\(|t| = 1\): a ratio. \(|t| = 2\): the double ratio of slide 7. A smooth gives a curve: evaluate on a grid of \(z\).

Upstream of the table: a penalty is not invariant to reparameterization, so the fit’s gauge also shapes \(\hat{\mu}\) itself. That is a modeling choice; the recipe takes \(\hat{\mu}\) as given.

From a black box

No term list. Assume every \(Q''\) reaches every variable outside \(T\).

Then the lemma says: no averaged table is mix-free. Partial dependence, ALE, model one-ways all move with the book, for every variable. The only mix-free object is the profile through the base cell.

Recipe — the same formula, with nothing optional about the pinning:

  1. Choose the base cell \(z^0\) deliberately. Every table depends on where every other variable sits, so use a well-populated cell and report it with the tables.
  2. Choose which tables the rating plan carries: main effects and the pairs you want.
  3. For each:

\[ \text{table}_t(z_t) = \prod_{u \subseteq t} \hat{\mu}\big(z^{(u)}\big)^{(-1)^{|t \setminus u|}} \]

  1. Check the truncation. With all \(2^{|V|}\) tables the product reproduces \(\hat{\mu}\) exactly; the tables you leave out are the residual. Compare \(\prod_{t\ \text{kept}} \text{table}_t(x_t)\) with \(\hat{\mu}(x)\) on the book.
